How our rankings get made

Updated: October 2026

The short answer: every PaisaSahi ranking follows the same three steps. (1) We list every major option — including issuers that don't pay us a rupee. (2) We score each one with a published formula, on identical standardized profiles. (3) We publish the order, the formula, and what each issuer pays us, side by side. A payout can never move a product up our list. If it could, the ranking would be an ad — and we'd label it as one.

What are the three steps?

List everyone

Every major option in the category goes on the table — including products from issuers we have no commercial relationship with. A ranking that only lists paying partners is a brochure.

Score the math

Each option is scored by a published formula on standardized user profiles. Same profiles, same assumptions, same arithmetic for everyone. No editorial "we just like this one" adjustments.

Publish everything

The order, the formula, the profiles, and what each issuer pays us — all on the page. If a high-paying product scores badly, it ranks badly, visibly.

What does the formula look like?

It varies by category, but the shape is always the same: net value to you, minus costs to you, computed on a realistic profile — never on headline claims. For credit cards:

Net value per year = (rewards at realistic earn rates × rupee value) + milestone benefits the profile can actually reach − annual fee − surcharges − forex markup
Illustrative example — check the issuer's current terms. Hypothetical card: ₹5,000 fee, 1% base rewards, capped fuel bonus worth ~₹1,900/year on a ₹25,000/month mixed-spend profile, no reachable milestones. Rewards ≈ ₹4,900 − fee ₹5,000 = −₹100/year. It ranks below a lifetime-free 1% card netting +₹3,000 on the same profile — regardless of what either issuer pays us.

How do you pick the standardized profiles?

Profiles are built from common real-world patterns — e.g., a ₹25,000/month mixed spender, a ₹60,000/month traveller — and published on every ranking page. We deliberately include profiles where "premium" products lose, because that's where the truth lives. If your spending doesn't match any profile, our calculators let you run the same formula on your own numbers.

How often do rankings get updated?

Every ranking is fully re-scored quarterly, and sooner if an issuer changes fees, reward rates, or caps. The "Updated" date at the top of each page shows the last full re-score — if a page looks stale, treat it as stale and tell us. Corrections are made publicly: the fix is noted on the page with a date, not buried.

What don't you rank?

Anything we can't score honestly. If a category's terms are too opaque to verify, or we lack the data to build a fair formula, we say so on the page instead of publishing a guess. A missing ranking is a feature — it means we didn't fake one.

We follow RBI and SEBI terminology for product definitions, so our categories mean the same thing the regulators mean.

Questions, answered

Do affiliate payouts affect your rankings?

No. Rankings are computed by the published formula before payouts enter the picture, and payouts are disclosed next to the results. A higher-paying product that scores worse ranks worse.

How often are rankings updated?

Quarterly at minimum, and immediately when an issuer changes material terms. The update date is always visible at the top of the page.

What if you get a ranking wrong?

We fix it in public — the correction is noted on the page with a date, and the underlying formula or data is updated. The error stays in the record.
