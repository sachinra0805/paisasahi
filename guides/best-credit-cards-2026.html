Best credit cards in India 2026 — ranked by math, not payouts

Updated: October 2026

The short answer: there is no single "best" credit card. The right card is the one with the highest net value for your spending — rewards plus milestone benefits, minus the annual fee and surcharges. We score every card with this formula on the same standardized spend profiles, publish the formula, and never let payouts change the order. A card that pays us more but scores worse ranks worse. Full stop.

How does PaisaSahi rank credit cards?

Three steps, same as every ranking we publish. First, we list every major card in the category — including cards from issuers that don't pay us anything. Second, we score each card with our net-value formula. Third, we publish the order, the formula, and what each issuer pays us, side by side.

Net value per year = (reward points × rupee value per point) + milestone benefits you'll actually reach − annual fee − fuel surcharges − forex markup on your foreign spends

The key phrase is you'll actually reach. A ₹10,000 milestone benefit on ₹8 lakh of annual spending is worth ₹0 to someone who spends ₹3 lakh. Most ranking sites count the headline benefit. We count what your profile can realistically unlock.

What spend profiles do you use?

We don't rank for a mythical "average Indian." We score every card against four standardized profiles, because a fuel-heavy driver and a frequent flyer need opposite cards:

A card can top one profile and flop on another. That's the point — the "best card" lists that name one winner for everyone are ranking for advertisers, not for you.

What does "net value" look like in practice?

Illustrative example — check the issuer's current terms. Take a hypothetical premium card: ₹5,000 annual fee, 1% base reward rate, bonus rewards on fuel capped at a low monthly limit, no milestone you'll reach. On the Everyday Spender profile (₹25,000/month = ₹3,00,000/year): base rewards ≈ ₹3,000, fuel bonus ≈ ₹1,900 (capped), milestones ₹0. Total rewards ≈ ₹4,900 − ₹5,000 fee = −₹100 per year. The card loses money. A lifetime-free 1% card on the same spending nets +₹3,000 with zero fee. The "premium" label cost this profile ₹3,100 a year.

This is the single most common trap in Indian credit cards: a fee that the rewards can't cover at your spending level. The fee is certain. The rewards are conditional.

Which cards should I be suspicious of?

Red flags we check before any card gets a good score:

  • High fee, low base earn rate. "Premium" in the name, 1% in the fine print. The fee is doing the marketing.
  • Reward caps that make the headline rate fiction. 5% cashback — capped at ₹500 a month. On real spending, the effective rate collapses.
  • Milestone benefits you'll never reach. Free flight vouchers at ₹8 lakh annual spend, sold to people spending ₹3 lakh.
  • Forex markup that eats travel rewards. A 3.5% markup on foreign spends wipes out a 2% reward rate — you pay 1.5% for the privilege.
  • Joining bonuses with spend conditions. ₹5,000 in vouchers if you spend ₹1 lakh in 60 days. That's not a gift, it's a spending target.

How do I choose a card in 10 minutes?

  1. Pull your last 3 months of actual spending and find your top 2 categories.
  2. Match the card to those categories — not to the lounge-access brochure.
  3. Compute net value: (rewards + reachable milestones) − (fee + surcharges).
  4. Read the caps. Then read them again.
  5. If net value is negative, take the lifetime-free card instead.
  6. Don't apply for three cards in one week — each application is a hard inquiry on your bureau file. CIBIL and the RBI's conduct rules for card issuers are worth knowing before you apply.

Questions, answered

Do the banks that pay you rank higher on PaisaSahi?

No. Every ranking is decided by the published net-value formula, computed on identical spend profiles. We disclose what each issuer pays us next to the ranking, and a payout cannot move a card up. If it could, the page would be an ad.

How often are your card rankings updated?

Quarterly, and sooner if an issuer changes fees, reward rates, or caps. The "Updated" date at the top always shows the last full re-score.

Should I hold multiple credit cards?

Two is the sweet spot for most people: one matched to your biggest spend category, one lifetime-free backup. Beyond three, management overhead and inquiry history start costing more than the rewards earn.

Will applying for a card hurt my CIBIL score?

Each application is a hard inquiry and can shave a few points temporarily; it fades in a few months. Space applications out — three in a week signals distress to lenders.

Is a lifetime-free card always better than a paid card?

Not always, but usually for moderate spenders. A paid card wins only when its extra rewards exceed its fee at your spending level. Run the formula; don't trust the brochure.
