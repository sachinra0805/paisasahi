The formula we use:
EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1)
where P = loan amount, r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = number of monthly payments. Reducing-balance, monthly rests — the same method Indian lenders use.
Assumptions: fixed interest rate for the full tenure; no prepayments; processing fees, GST, and loan insurance are not included (ask your lender for the all-in cost). Floating-rate loans will differ as rates move with the RBI's repo rate — see rbi.org.in for the current policy rate.

How is EMI calculated?

Every EMI has two parts: interest on what you still owe, plus a slice of principal. In the early years the outstanding balance is high, so most of your EMI is interest. Over time the balance falls, the interest portion shrinks, and more of each payment attacks the principal. Same EMI every month, quietly changing composition underneath.

What moves the needle most?

Tenure is the silent killer. Stretching a home loan from 20 to 30 years drops the EMI — but can add 60–70% more total interest. A 1% lower rate on a 20-year ₹50 lakh loan saves roughly ₹7–8 lakh in interest. Before comparing lenders, compare these two levers; the processing-fee difference is usually noise.

Common questions

How is EMI calculated?

On a reducing-balance basis: EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly interest rate, and n the number of instalments. Interest is charged only on the outstanding principal each month.

Does prepaying my loan actually help?

Yes. Prepayments cut the principal that future interest is computed on, so early prepayments save the most. Even one extra EMI a year can shave years off a long loan. Check for prepayment penalties first — floating-rate home loans in India typically have none.

Fixed vs floating rate — which is better?

Fixed gives certainty; floating moves with the RBI repo rate and usually starts lower. Neither wins universally — pick fixed if you need predictable payments, floating if you can ride rate cycles.

Why is my early EMI mostly interest?

Because the outstanding principal is highest at the start, the interest slice of each EMI is largest then. It's normal amortisation — and exactly why prepaying early saves the most interest.
