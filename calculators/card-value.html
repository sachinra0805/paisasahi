Is your credit card actually worth its annual fee?

A card charging ₹3,000 a year needs to return more than ₹3,000 in rewards. This checker runs that math on your real spending — using reward rates you set, not the bank's marketing.

The one-line answer: a credit card earns its fee only when (your spending × your reward rate, summed across categories) − annual fee comes out positive. Enter your numbers below — the verdict updates live.

The formula:
Net value = Σ (monthly spend × 12 × reward rate) − annual fee, summed across the five categories.
Reward rate = effective % value back per rupee as you would redeem it — e.g. if 1 point = ₹0.25 in statement credit, a "4 points per ₹100" earn is a 1% rate.
Illustrative example — check the issuer's current terms.
Meera spends ₹10,000/month on groceries and assumes a 2% reward rate: ₹10,000 × 12 × 2% = ₹2,400/year in rewards. On a card with a ₹1,500 annual fee, her net value is ₹900 — the card pays for itself. Drop her assumed rate to 1% and the net falls to −₹300: it doesn't.

How do I figure out a card's real reward rate?

Take the issuer's rewards chart and convert points to rupees using your redemption. Statement credit is the honest baseline: if 1 point = ₹0.25 there, a "5X points" earn is 1.25% back. Airline miles can be worth more — or far less — depending on the redemption. When in doubt, use the statement-credit value; it's the floor nobody can argue with.

When does an annual-fee card make sense?

When the net value stays positive year after year on spending you'd do anyway — or when perks you'd genuinely pay for (lounge visits you actually take, subscriptions you'd actually buy) exceed the fee on their own. Run this checker with the fee set to zero versus the real fee: if the card only wins because of a first-year welcome bonus, it's a one-year card, not a keeper.

What about fee waivers on spend milestones?

Treat the fee as zero only if you'd cross the waiver threshold with normal spending. A ₹5,000 fee waived at ₹4 lakh annual spend is free for someone spending ₹5 lakh — and a ₹5,000 fee for someone spending ₹2.5 lakh. Don't inflate your spending to chase a waiver; that's the bank's game, not yours.

Questions, answered

How do I figure out a card's real reward rate?

Take the issuer's rewards chart and convert points to rupees using how you would actually redeem them. If 1 point equals ₹0.25 as statement credit, a "5X points" earn is really 1.25% back. Ignore the marketing multiplier and do this division yourself.

When does an annual-fee credit card make sense?

When the net value stays positive year after year on your normal spending, or when perks you would pay for anyway exceed the fee. One-time welcome bonuses should not decide it, because they do not repeat.

What about fee waivers on spend milestones?

Treat the fee as zero only if you would hit the waiver threshold with spending you would do anyway. If the waiver needs ₹3 lakh of annual spending and you spend ₹2 lakh, you are paying the fee.

Do milestone benefits count in the value calculation?

Only if you would use them without changing your behavior. A voucher worth ₹5,000 that you would never have bought is worth ₹0 to you.
