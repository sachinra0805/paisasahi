How we compute it:
New taxable income = gross salary − ₹75,000 standard deduction (no other deductions allowed).
Old taxable income = gross salary − ₹50,000 standard deduction − 80C (capped ₹1.5L) − 80D − HRA/other.
Slab tax is applied marginally to each band, then: new regime gets the 87A rebate (zero tax up to ₹12L taxable) with marginal relief just above ₹12L; old regime gets the ₹12,500 87A rebate up to ₹5L taxable. Finally, 4% health & education cess is added to the tax.
Assumptions — read these: individual taxpayer below 60 years; salaried income only (standard deduction applied); 4% cess included; surcharge not modelled (matters only above ₹50L income); HRA taken at face value — the actual HRA exemption needs a rent-based computation; other deductions like 80TTA, NPS (80CCD), and home-loan interest are not separate inputs (fold them into "HRA + other"); marginal relief applied in simplified form. Verify your final liability on incometax.gov.in.

What are the slabs for FY 2026-27?

Budget 2026 (presented 1 February 2026) made no changes to income-tax slabs under either regime. FY 2026-27 (assessment year 2027-28) carries forward the structure Budget 2025 set for FY 2025-26 — now under the Income-tax Act, 2025 instead of the 1961 Act. So which regime should you pick?

The new regime's lower slabs usually beat the old regime's deductions — until your deductions get big. The crossover sits around ₹4 lakh of total deductions for most salary levels. Big HRA (metro renters), home-loan interest, and maxed-out 80C + 80D + NPS are what push people back to the old regime. If you're near the line, the honest answer is: compute both every year, because salary hikes and rent changes move it.

Common questions

Which regime is better for salaried employees in FY 2026-27?

Usually the new regime — lower slabs and zero tax up to ₹12L taxable income. The old regime wins when total deductions (80C + 80D + HRA + home-loan interest) cross roughly ₹4 lakh. Run your numbers above; it takes ten seconds.

Is the new regime the default?

Yes, since FY 2023-24 (now section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025). Salaried taxpayers can opt for the old regime each year while filing — just inform your employer so TDS is deducted correctly.

What is marginal relief in the new regime?

If your taxable income is just above ₹12 lakh, the extra tax can't exceed the amount by which you cross ₹12 lakh. Earning a little more never leaves you worse off — the relief phases out naturally as income rises.

Do I submit investment proofs under the new regime?

No — there are almost no deductions to claim, so no proofs needed. Under the old regime, every deduction you claim needs documentary proof submitted to your employer.
