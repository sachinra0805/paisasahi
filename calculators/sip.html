The formula we use:
FV = M × [((1+r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1+r)
where M = monthly investment, r = monthly return (annual ÷ 12 ÷ 100), n = number of months. This is the future value of a monthly annuity with payments at the start of each month.
Assumptions — read these: returns are assumed constant every month; real markets are volatile and this smooths all of that away. Figures are before fund expense ratios, exit loads, and taxes on gains. This is a scenario illustrator, not a prediction. For scheme details, check amfiindia.com and sebi.gov.in.

What is a SIP, really?

A Systematic Investment Plan puts a fixed sum into a mutual fund every month. When markets fall you automatically buy more units; when they rise you buy fewer. That rupee-cost averaging is the entire magic — no timing, no tips, just consistency. It's boring, which is why it works.

Why time matters more than timing

Compounding needs years, not cleverness. A ₹10,000 SIP at an assumed 12% becomes ~₹23 lakh in 10 years — but ~₹50 lakh in 15 years and ~₹1 crore in 20 years, on only 1.5–2× the invested amount. The gains curve bends upward late. Starting five years earlier beats picking a slightly better fund, almost every time.

Common questions

What is a SIP?

A fixed amount invested at regular intervals — usually monthly — into a mutual fund. You get rupee-cost averaging automatically: more units when markets are low, fewer when high.

Is the return guaranteed?

No. Mutual funds are market-linked; returns vary and can be negative over short periods. This calculator assumes a constant return purely for illustration. Anyone guaranteeing equity returns is selling something.

What is a step-up SIP?

Increasing your SIP by a fixed percentage (often 10%) each year, roughly matching salary growth. The increases compound, so a step-up SIP builds a far larger corpus than a flat one for modestly higher total investment.

What return should I assume?

Many planners illustrate long-horizon equity SIPs at 10–12% p.a. — an assumption, not a forecast. Run a second scenario 2–3% lower and make sure your plan survives that one too.
