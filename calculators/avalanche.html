Debt Avalanche Clearance

The order you clear debts in changes what they cost you — by lakhs, sometimes. List every debt, add one extra monthly payment, and watch the plan build itself.

Updated: October 2026
The one-line answer: pay the minimum on everything, then aim every spare rupee at the highest interest rate — not the biggest loan, not the loudest lender. That's the avalanche, and it's the cheapest order there is.

    How the simulation works: month by month, each debt first accrues interest (balance × APR ÷ 12). Every debt then gets its minimum payment. The extra pool — your extra payment plus the minimums of every debt already cleared — goes to the target debt (highest APR for avalanche, smallest balance for snowball), cascading to the next debt if the target clears mid-month. The "minimums only" comparison keeps paying each minimum until that debt clears, with no roll-over.
    Assumptions: fixed APRs and fixed minimum payments for the whole period; no new borrowing; no prepayment penalties or charges. Real cards vary the minimum due with the balance — if yours does, use a realistic average payment. Rates that float will move the dates, not the logic.

    Why the order matters this much

    A rupee of extra payment kills interest at the rate of the debt it lands on. Put ₹10,000 extra against a 42% credit card and it saves ₹4,200 a year in interest. Put the same ₹10,000 against a 9% home loan and it saves ₹900. Same money, same month — nearly five times the effect. The avalanche is simply the discipline of always landing the extra rupee where the rate is highest.

    The honest case for the snowball

    Snowball — smallest balance first — always costs a bit more interest. What it buys is an early win: a debt gone entirely, one less bill each month. If you've started and abandoned payoff plans before, that momentum may be worth more than the interest difference. Run both strategies above; when the gap is small, pick the one you'll still be following in month six.

    Common questions

    What is the debt avalanche method?

    Pay the minimum on every debt, and throw every spare rupee at the highest interest rate. When it clears, roll its full payment into the next-highest rate. It's the order that costs the least total interest.

    Avalanche or snowball — which is better?

    Avalanche always costs less interest. Snowball can clear a debt sooner, which keeps some people going. The best plan is the one you don't abandon.

    What if my minimum doesn't cover the interest?

    That debt grows every month and can never clear — this page warns you when it happens. Raise that payment above the monthly interest, even slightly, or ask the lender about restructuring.

    Should I include my home loan?

    You can. Most people avalanche the expensive debts first and treat the home loan separately, since its rate is lowest and its tenure longest. Try it both ways and compare the interest line.

    Debt first, or emergency fund first?

    A small cushion first — about a month of expenses — so a surprise doesn't go straight back on the card. Then the avalanche. Grow the full fund after the costly debt is gone.
