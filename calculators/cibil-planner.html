Your 90-day CIBIL improvement plan

Five questions about your credit situation. In return: a phased action plan — what to fix in days 1–30, 31–60, and 61–90. Built from simple, published rules, not a black box.

The one-line answer: most score improvement comes from three levers — keep utilization under 30%, never miss a payment, and let old accounts age. This planner turns your situation into a dated to-do list for each lever.

How the plan is built (the rules, published):
Utilization above 30% → paydown actions land in Phase 1. Any missed payment in 12 months → autopay + "no new applications" actions. Zero active accounts → the credit-building path (secured/FD-backed card). Oldest account under 1 year → keep-old-cards actions. Score below 650 (or unknown) → dispute-errors-first. Everything not triggered becomes maintenance.
Assumptions & limits: this is an educational tool, not a guarantee — nobody can promise a specific score by a specific date. Scores move on bureau update cycles: lenders typically report monthly, so allow 30–60 days to see utilization changes. Disputes with bureaus take additional weeks. If a "score improvement agent" promises otherwise for a fee, walk away.

How fast can my CIBIL score actually improve?

It depends which lever you're pulling. Utilization is the fast one: pay down balances and the improvement usually shows within one or two bureau cycles — roughly 30–60 days, since lenders report monthly. Payment history is the slow one: a missed payment can weigh on your report for years, though its sting fades as newer clean months pile up. Anyone promising "+100 points in 30 days, guaranteed" is selling you something.

Does checking my own score hurt it?

No — this is the most persistent myth in Indian personal finance. Checking your own report is a soft inquiry and leaves your score untouched. What dings you (slightly, temporarily) are hard inquiries: lender-initiated checks when you apply for a card or loan. So check freely, but apply selectively — five card applications in a month looks desperate to the algorithm, because it is.

What counts as a "good" score?

CIBIL scores run 300–900. As a rule of thumb Indian lenders use: 750+ is strong (approvals, better rates), 700–750 is workable, 650–700 gets harder, and below 650 means most prime lenders will decline or price you steeply. These bands are the industry's, not ours — and different lenders draw their lines differently.

Questions, answered

How fast can my CIBIL score improve?

Utilization changes usually show up within one or two bureau update cycles — roughly 30 to 60 days, since lenders report monthly. Payment-history damage takes longer: a missed payment can weigh on your report for years, though its impact fades with time.

Does checking my own CIBIL score hurt it?

No. Checking your own report is a soft inquiry and does not affect your score. Only lender-initiated checks when you apply for credit (hard inquiries) can cause a small, temporary dip.

What is a good CIBIL score in India?

CIBIL scores run from 300 to 900. Lenders generally treat 750 and above as a strong score for approvals at better terms. Below 650, approvals get noticeably harder and costlier.

Will closing an old credit card help my score?

Usually the opposite. Closing a card raises your utilization ratio because you lose that credit limit, and it shortens your average account age over time. Keep old no-fee cards open with occasional small use.

How often should I check my credit report?

Quarterly is plenty for most people. You are entitled to one free full credit report per year from each credit bureau in India — space them across bureaus to keep a free eye on your file year-round.
