Credit Card Truth: APR & Fees

Cards are sold on points and lounges. They're priced in a monthly percentage almost nobody annualises, a minimum due designed to stretch your debt for years, and a fee stack printed in a document most people never open. Let's open it.

Updated: October 2026
The one-line answer: a "3.5% per month" card charges an effective 51.1% per year — and paying only the minimum due on ₹1 lakh at that rate takes 18+ years and over ₹2 lakh of interest.

1 · The APR converter

Your card's terms quote a monthly rate. Here's what it really is per year.

Effective annual rate (the true APR)
51.1%
Nominal APR (monthly × 12)42.0%

The effective rate is higher than monthly × 12 because each month's interest itself earns interest. Add 18% GST on the interest, which issuers charge on top.

2 · The minimum-due trap

The minimum due feels like a payment plan. It isn't — it's a slow-drip. Simulate paying only the minimum, every month, no new spending.

The way out isn't a bigger minimum — it's a fixed payment. Take the first month's minimum and keep paying that same rupee amount every month; the balance falls instead of the payment. Or run your cards through the Avalanche planner.

The formulas we use:
Nominal APR: monthly % × 12  ·  Effective annual rate: ((1 + monthly ÷ 100)12 − 1) × 100
Trap simulation: month by month — interest accrues at APR ÷ 12 on the balance, then you pay max(minimum % of balance, floor), capped at the balance. No new spending, no fees, no GST in the simulation — reality is worse.

3 · The common fee stack

Every card carries the same family of charges, whatever the branding. These are typical ranges across mass-market Indian cards — indicative, not facts about any specific card. Issuers differ, slabs change, and your card's MITC (Most Important Terms & Conditions) is the only exact source.

Honesty note: we publish ranges here, not exact card-by-card fees, because exact figures change and vary by card variant. Check your own card's MITC for exact figures — your issuer is required to provide it, and it's the document that governs what you'll actually be charged.

How to use a card without feeding it

Three habits neutralise almost everything on this page: pay the full statement amount, not the minimum or the "total due minus a bit"; never withdraw cash on the card; and treat the interest-free period as a perk that exists only while the first habit holds. Do those and the fee stack shrinks to the annual fee — which is exactly the number our Card Value Checker tests against your rewards.

Common questions

What is credit card APR in India?

Cards quote interest monthly, commonly ~3%–4.2%. Nominal APR is monthly × 12; the effective annual rate is higher due to monthly compounding — 3.5%/month is 42% nominal but about 51.1% effective.

How does the minimum-due trap work?

The minimum due is usually a small % of the outstanding, so your payment shrinks as the balance shrinks — stretching repayment for years. On ₹1 lakh at 42% APR, minimum-only payments can take 18+ years and cost over ₹2 lakh in interest.

When is a card actually interest-free?

Only when the previous statement was paid in full by the due date. Carry anything forward and new purchases typically accrue interest from the day of purchase.

Are these fee figures exact for my card?

No — they're typical ranges, labelled as such. Your exact figures live in your card's MITC document. Any site quoting one exact fee for every card is making it up.
